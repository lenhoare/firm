# Delegation — working hypotheses

The aim is useful work from the whole team within a modest allowance. Codex/Astra provides direction and review, but any model can identify a better approach, spot a mistaken assumption, or contribute an idea the manager missed. These principles are starting points to vary through experience.

## Plan progressively

Create an overall checklist of outcomes and divide it into coherent phases. Detail the next phase enough to delegate it; keep later phases provisional until earlier work tests the assumptions behind them. A large checklist is useful when it exposes dependencies and omissions, but its size is not a goal.

Track accepted outcomes. A worker finishing makes an assignment ready for review; evidence that it meets its criteria makes it complete. Slow progress should trigger investigation, clarification, or a smaller assignment. Elapsed time alone is not a reason to declare success or abandon necessary work.

## Give enough context to exercise judgment

Each assignment should explain the intended result, why it matters, relevant files and examples, established decisions, constraints, completion checks, and when to report a blocker. Distinguish firm requirements from the manager’s suggested approach. Make additional context accessible when needed without requiring every worker to absorb the entire project history.

Route work by how clearly it can be specified and verified, alongside difficulty and observed model strengths. A substantial, contained implementation may delegate well; a small but ambiguous architectural decision may need more manager involvement.

## Vary the degree of control

Treat autonomy as a per-assignment choice, adjustable by Len and the manager:

- **Guided:** a narrow change with a suggested method and close review.
- **Bounded autonomy:** a clear outcome and constraints; the worker chooses implementation details and verifies its result.
- **Exploratory:** a specific question, time or usage allowance, and a request for alternatives, evidence, or a small prototype.

Task size, brief detail, and review frequency can change independently. More detailed instructions are not automatically better: they cost manager effort and can prevent useful discovery. Broaden autonomy where results justify it; narrow or clarify assignments when rework suggests a mismatch. Any autonomy level remains subject to controller allowances and the authorised project scope.

## Make disagreement useful

Invite workers to challenge assumptions and suggest improvements. Their result can include an optional observation: what they noticed, the proposed alternative, supporting evidence, expected benefit, and any effect on scope or interfaces. Do not require an idea quota.

Workers can adopt improvements within their delegated discretion and explain them afterward. Changes to shared contracts, scope, or established decisions should become proposals for review before dependent work proceeds. An incorrect premise that blocks the assignment should be reported promptly.

Persist proposals with their originating model and task. Review ordinary suggestions alongside completed work to avoid a manager turn for every idea. Record whether each proposal was adopted, deferred, or declined and why. Judge it on evidence, using a small experiment when useful; the manager’s original plan is revisable. Incorporate accepted discoveries into shared context and future assignments.

## Learn from the cost of collaboration

Record first-review acceptance, clarification rounds, rework, manager usage where available, and useful worker discoveries. Compare similar tasks while varying a few delegation choices at a time. Keep these as observations rather than a universal model ranking.

When delegation struggles, examine the brief, missing context, task boundaries, and acceptance criteria as well as the worker’s capability. Limit repeated repair loops: the manager may reframe, reassign, handle the difficult part directly, or bring a decision to Len.

The hypothesis to test is that good decomposition and appropriate autonomy reduce total manager effort while preserving quality and contributions from every model.
